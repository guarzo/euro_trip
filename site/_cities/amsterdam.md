## In winter

January highs are around 6&nbsp;°C (43&nbsp;°F) — **the coldest city on this list** — and **the sun sets at about 4:29&nbsp;PM in late December.** It rains often, and the wind comes off the water with nothing to stop it. Cycling, which is how the city actually works, is unpleasant in horizontal January rain.

**This is the one city here rated mixed rather than good, and it earns that rating honestly.** Cold, dark, and wet is the accurate summary, and three nights in it is a real commitment.

The counterweight is specific and unusually good: the **Amsterdam Light Festival** runs roughly from late November to mid-January, with commissioned illuminated artworks installed along the canals and viewed by boat or on foot. It is a rare case of an event that works *because* it gets dark at half past four — the festival would be impossible in summer, when it never really gets dark at all. A heated canal boat through the installations on a cold night is genuinely one of the better evenings available anywhere in this plan.

The museums do the rest of the work. The Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum are both first-rate and both entirely indoors.

## Why go

The Rijksmuseum holds Rembrandt's *Night Watch* and the best Vermeers, in a building that is itself worth the ticket. The Van Gogh Museum is the largest collection of his work anywhere, arranged chronologically so you watch the style resolve into itself over about a decade.

The Anne Frank House is the other reason. It is the actual annex, entered through the actual bookcase, and it lands hard — particularly for teenagers who have read the diary at school. **Tickets are timed, released on a fixed schedule ahead of the date, and sell out within hours.** This is the single booking on this trip most likely to be missed by not planning far enough ahead.

The city is also the most walkable on this list. The canal ring is compact, flat, and coherent, and you can cross the center on foot in half an hour.

The honest counterpoint, beyond the weather: Amsterdam's center is small and heavily visited, and the Red Light District and coffee shops make parts of it a stag-party destination that families navigate around rather than through. It is manageable — the canal ring is large and most of it is quiet — but worth knowing in advance.

## Eat

- **Stroopwafels** — pressed fresh at a market stall, warm, with the syrup still soft. Not the packaged kind.
- **Bitterballen** — crumbed, deep-fried ragout balls, served with mustard alongside a drink.
- **Rijsttafel** — the Indonesian "rice table," a dozen or more small dishes at once. A legacy of Dutch colonial history and the best group meal in the city.
- **Herring** — raw, with onions, eaten from the tail. Genuinely good and not everyone will try it.
- **Appeltaart** — thick Dutch apple pie with whipped cream, in a brown café, on a cold afternoon.

## Getting here / onward

Schiphol (AMS) is one of Europe's major hubs, with extensive direct US service, and the train to Centraal takes about 15 minutes.

Onward by rail: Paris about 3h20 and London about 4 hours, both by Eurostar; Brussels about 2 hours. Amsterdam is the natural start or end point of the northern arc, since Schiphol makes an open-jaw booking easy from either direction.
