## In winter

January highs are around 11&nbsp;°C (52&nbsp;°F), colder than coastal Spain — Madrid sits on a plateau at roughly 650&nbsp;m, and the air is thin and dry. What that buys you is sunshine: it is frequently bright and clear when [Barcelona]({{ '/cities/barcelona/' | relative_url }}) is grey, and sunset is not until about 5:55&nbsp;PM. Cold in the shade, pleasant in the sun, and almost never wet for long.

The Plaza Mayor Christmas market runs through early January. The bigger draw is **New Year's Eve at Puerta del Sol**, where the entire country watches the clock and eats twelve grapes on the twelve strokes of midnight — one of the few genuinely distinctive holiday experiences available on this trip. It is also extremely crowded; the square fills hours ahead.

## Why go

Three of the best art museums in the world sit within a few blocks of each other. The Prado alone holds the Velázquezes and the Goyas, including the black paintings, and the Reina Sofía a short walk away holds *Guernica*. If anyone in the family cares about painting, this is the densest concentration of it anywhere on the list.

Madrid is also the most straightforwardly *livable* city here — big, unhurried, with proper parks and a nightlife that starts around the time other cities are finishing. Dinner at 10&nbsp;PM is normal, which some travelers love and others find exhausting.

The honest counterpoint: Madrid has no single unmissable landmark. There is no Colosseum, no Sagrada Família, no Acropolis. It rewards people who enjoy being in a city more than people who want to tick off monuments, and it is worth being honest about which the family is.

## Eat

- **Cocido madrileño** — a long-simmered chickpea stew served in courses. A winter dish, in season now.
- **Bocadillo de calamares** — fried squid in a bread roll, sold near Plaza Mayor, and inexplicably correct.
- **Churros at San Ginés** — open absurdly late, and the traditional stop after a night out.
- **Tapas in La Latina** — jamón, tortilla, croquetas, one bar at a time.
- **Twelve grapes at midnight** — if you are here for New Year's, this is compulsory and harder than it sounds.

## Getting here / onward

Barajas (MAD) has direct US service year-round and is the main hub for Latin American connections; the metro reaches the center in about 30 minutes.

Onward by rail, all high-speed: Barcelona about 2.5 hours, Seville about 2.5 hours, Granada about 3.5 hours. Madrid's central rail position makes it the natural hub for any Spain-heavy version of this trip.
