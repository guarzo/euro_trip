## In winter

January highs are around 8&nbsp;°C (47&nbsp;°F). **The sun sets at about 3:53&nbsp;PM around the solstice — the earliest darkness of any city on this list, and the single strongest argument against the northern arc.** By four in the afternoon it is night. Add frequent drizzle and low cloud, and a December day here gives you maybe six hours of usable grey light.

That is the honest headline. The compensations are unusually strong, though, and they are structural rather than seasonal.

**The major museums are free.** The British Museum, the National Gallery, Tate Modern, the Natural History Museum, and the V&A all have free general admission. That changes how you plan a wet day: you can walk into the National Gallery for forty minutes, leave when attention runs out, and come back the next day, without any of it feeling wasted. No other city here allows that.

Hyde Park's Winter Wonderland typically runs from late November into early January, and the West End is in full pantomime-and-blockbuster season.

## Why go

For an 18- and a 17-year-old, London is probably the highest-appeal city on this list. West End theatre at a standard nowhere else matches, Camden and Shoreditch, record shops and vintage markets, and — not a small thing on a two-week trip through five languages — **no language barrier at all.** After ten days of pointing at menus, that is a genuine relief for everyone.

The museums are also the best rainy-day insurance available anywhere in this plan.

The honest counterpoint, beyond the dark: London is large and spread out in a way the other cities are not. Getting between things takes real time on the Underground, and four nights buys you fewer distinct experiences than four nights in Rome or Barcelona would. It is also outside the Schengen area, which makes it a separate border crossing rather than an internal hop.

## Eat

- **Sunday roast** — beef, Yorkshire pudding, gravy. Book a pub that takes it seriously.
- **Indian food** — genuinely a London specialty rather than a novelty; some of the best outside the subcontinent.
- **Borough Market stalls** — grazing rather than a meal.
- **Full English breakfast** — once, as an experience, ideally before a long walking day.
- **Fish and chips** — from a proper chip shop, wrapped, with vinegar. Not from a pub menu.

## Getting here / onward

Heathrow (LHR) has the most direct US service of any airport on this list; the Piccadilly line reaches the center in about 50 minutes and the Elizabeth line is faster. Gatwick, Stansted, and Luton handle most budget traffic.

Onward: **St Pancras to Paris by Eurostar in 2h20**, or Amsterdam in about 4 hours, both city center to city center. Note the border: the UK is not in the Schengen area, so entering and leaving is a separate immigration process with its own requirements — see [ETIAS, passports, and paperwork]({{ '/questions/etias-and-passports/' | relative_url }}).
