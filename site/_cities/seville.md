## In winter

January highs are around 16&nbsp;°C (61&nbsp;°F) and the sun does not set until about 6:00&nbsp;PM. **This is the warmest and brightest option anywhere in this plan** — roughly two more hours of usable afternoon per day than London, and shirtsleeves weather by midday more often than not.

Seville's problem is the opposite season: summer here regularly passes 40&nbsp;°C (104&nbsp;°F) and the city empties out at midday. Winter is simply when this city is at its best, with no qualification.

Low season also means the Real Alcázar is walkable rather than a shuffle, and the cathedral queue is short. The bitter orange trees lining every street are in fruit — the fruit is inedible and mostly ends up as British marmalade, but the streets look extraordinary.

## Why go

The Real Alcázar is the finest surviving example of Mudéjar architecture — built by Muslim craftsmen for Christian kings, layered over five centuries, and still a working royal residence. The cathedral next door is the largest Gothic church in the world by volume, with Columbus's tomb inside and the Giralda, a former minaret, as its bell tower.

Flamenco here is not a floor show. It is a local art form with a serious circuit, and a small tablao is one of the more affecting evenings available on this trip.

The honest counterpoint: two days covers Seville comfortably, and it is a long way south. Adding it means committing to Spain as the trip's center of gravity rather than treating it as one stop among several countries.

## Eat

- **Jamón ibérico de bellota** — acorn-fed, aged for years, and the version worth the ceremony.
- **Salmorejo** — thicker and richer than gazpacho, topped with egg and ham.
- **Espinacas con garbanzos** — spinach and chickpeas, a Moorish holdover, and the standard vegetarian tapa.
- **Sherry** — from nearby Jerez. Bone-dry fino is the local pour, and nothing like the sweet stuff the name suggests.
- **Naranjas amargas** — the street oranges. Do not eat one; the family will insist on trying anyway.

## Getting here / onward

Seville's airport handles mostly domestic and European traffic; most arrivals come by high-speed train.

Onward by rail: Madrid about 2.5 hours by AVE. Granada is about 2.5 hours by train or bus, which makes pairing the two Andalusian cities straightforward if the trip goes deep into southern Spain.
