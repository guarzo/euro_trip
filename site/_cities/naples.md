## In winter

January highs run about 13&nbsp;°C (55&nbsp;°F), the mildest of any Italian city here. The bay moderates everything; it rains, but it rarely gets properly cold.

Winter suits Naples unusually well for two reasons. **Pompeii** is open year-round and is far more bearable in cool weather — the site is large, almost entirely exposed, and walking it in August heat is genuinely punishing. And **Via San Gregorio Armeno**, the narrow street of workshops making nativity figures, runs all year but peaks in December, when it becomes one of the more distinctive things happening anywhere on this list. The craftsmen add new figures each year, including satirical ones of public figures, which is half the entertainment.

## Why go

Pizza was invented here and is still better here than anywhere. The Archaeological Museum holds the mosaics and frescoes actually lifted from Pompeii, which means the museum and the ruins are two halves of one thing — see both or the visit is incomplete.

**The honest part: Naples is chaotic, grimy in places, and not to everyone's taste.** The traffic is anarchic, the street noise is constant, and parts of the center are visibly rundown. People who love it love it precisely for that; people who want an orderly, pretty Italian city should go to [Florence]({{ '/cities/florence/' | relative_url }}) instead. It is worth knowing which camp the family is in before committing two nights.

## Where to stay

**Chiaia** is the calm, safe, seafront choice, with the Villa Comunale gardens as a buffer from the noise — but it is a bus ride or a 20-minute walk from Spaccanapoli and Via San Gregorio Armeno, which are most of this page's Day 1.

**Centro Storico**, near Piazza del Plebiscito, puts you on top of Spaccanapoli, the nativity workshops, and the Archaeological Museum, at the cost of the noise and grit this page already admits is part of Naples. For two nights, staying inside the chaos rather than commuting to it is usually the better trade.

## Eat

- **Pizza margherita** — at its point of origin. The dough is soft and wet in the center; that is correct, not undercooked.
- **Sfogliatella** — layered shell pastry with ricotta, eaten warm at the counter.
- **Pizza fritta** — fried, folded, stuffed. Street food, and the version most visitors never try.
- **Espresso** — served short, hot, and already sweetened unless you say otherwise.
- **Babà** — rum-soaked sponge. Not subtle.

## Getting here / onward

Naples Capodichino (NAP) handles mostly European traffic; US arrivals usually connect through Rome or another hub.

Onward by rail: Rome about 1.2 hours by high-speed train, which makes Naples an easy add-on to a Rome stay rather than a separate expedition. Note that the Amalfi Coast and Capri are a summer proposition — boat connections thin out and much of Positano and Capri close for the winter, which is why they are on the [ruled-out list]({{ '/ruled-out/' | relative_url }}) rather than in this sketch.
