## In winter

January highs are about 12&nbsp;°C (54&nbsp;°F) in the city, cooler than [Seville]({{ '/cities/seville/' | relative_url }}) because Granada sits higher and inland, against the mountains. Sunset is around 6:00&nbsp;PM — the same late southern light.

Winter is when Granada looks the way it does on postcards: **the Alhambra in the foreground and the Sierra Nevada behind it under snow.** That image is specifically a cold-weather one. In summer the mountains are bare and brown.

Everything stays open. The Alhambra's winter hours are shorter, and the gardens are less floral than in spring, but the architecture — which is the reason to come — is unaffected.

## Why go

The Alhambra is the best-preserved medieval Islamic palace complex in the world, and unlike most such superlatives it is not arguable. The Nasrid Palaces are carved plaster and tile at a density that photographs cannot convey, and the Generalife gardens above them were built around moving water in a way that still works.

Two things make Granada unusually good for this family in particular. **Free tapas**: Granada is among the last Spanish cities where an actual plate of food arrives free with every drink ordered, and it escalates as the evening goes on — teenagers find this delightful and it makes an evening out effectively self-catering. And **the Sierra Nevada ski resort is roughly 45 minutes from the city center**, which puts a real ski day on the table if Bubu or Gaby want one. That is worth raising directly on [what the kids want]({{ '/questions/what-kids-want/' | relative_url }}).

The honest counterpoint: Granada is small, and once the Alhambra is done the city is a pleasant evening rather than a second full day of sights. Two nights is right; three would be padding unless you ski.

## Eat

- **Free tapas** — the city's defining custom. What arrives is the bar's choice, which is half the fun.
- **Piononos** — small rolled sponge cakes soaked in syrup, from nearby Santa Fe.
- **Habas con jamón** — broad beans with ham, the local standby.
- **Moorish tea houses** — the *teterías* on Calle Calderería, serving mint tea and pastries in rooms styled after Morocco.
- **Tortilla del Sacromonte** — an omelette traditionally made with offal. Ask before ordering blind.

## Getting here / onward

Granada's airport is small, with domestic and limited European connections; most visitors arrive by rail or road.

Onward: Madrid about 3.5 hours by train, Seville about 2.5 hours by train or bus. Granada is the deepest point of any southern Spain itinerary — worth planning as the far end of a loop rather than a stop along the way.
