## In winter

January highs sit around 12&nbsp;°C (54&nbsp;°F), with the sun setting near 4:40&nbsp;PM in late December. Cool, often wet, rarely cold enough to be miserable.

Nothing important closes. That matters more here than anywhere else on the list, because Rome's problem in summer is not weather but volume — the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums in July are a slow-moving crush. In January you walk in. Same buildings, a fraction of the people. Of every city we are considering, Rome is the one most improved by going in winter.

The Piazza Navona Christmas market runs through early January. Rain is the real nuisance, and Rome handles it well: the Pantheon, the Borghese, and the Capitoline Museums are all indoor, all excellent, and all within reach of a wet afternoon.

## Why go

It is the densest concentration of things worth seeing anywhere on this list. You can walk from a 2,000-year-old temple to a Bernini fountain to the best carbonara of your life inside twenty minutes, and the walk itself is the point.

Four nights is the right number. Three works but you will feel it; a day is lost to the Vatican alone, and the Forum deserves a slow morning rather than a forced march.

The honest counterpoint: Rome is busy, loud, and relentlessly hard-surfaced. There is not much green, the traffic is aggressive, and by day three the beauty can start to feel like homework. Build in an afternoon with nothing scheduled.

## Eat

- **Cacio e pepe and carbonara** — Roman pasta is four dishes done perfectly. Order them where the menu is short.
- **Supplì** — fried rice balls with a molten mozzarella core. The standard street snack.
- **Pizza al taglio** — by the slice, sold by weight, eaten standing up.
- **Gelato** — good year-round, and the queues in January are nonexistent.
- **Carciofi alla giudia** — flattened deep-fried artichokes from the Jewish Quarter. Winter is artichoke season, so this is one dish that is genuinely better now.

## Getting here / onward

Fiumicino (FCO) has direct US service year-round; the Leonardo Express runs to Termini in about 32 minutes. Ciampino handles mostly intra-European budget traffic.

Onward by rail: Florence about 1.5 hours, Naples about 1.2 hours — both high-speed, both city center to city center, both faster than flying once airports are counted. By air: Barcelona or Paris about 2 hours, Athens about 2 hours.
