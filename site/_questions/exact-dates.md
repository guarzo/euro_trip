## Why it matters

"Winter break 2028/29" covers about three weeks, and the two ends of it are genuinely different trips. Christmas week is festive, crowded, and expensive; mid-January is quiet, cheap, and grey. Both are defensible. Picking one determines what we book and how far ahead.

**A caveat that matters:** school calendars for 2028/29 are not published yet. Everything below is provisional until we know the actual break dates, and this question cannot be closed before then. What we *can* do now is agree on which end of the break we want, so that when the calendar lands we already know what to aim at.

## The options

### 1. The Christmas window — roughly 20 December to 3 January

Markets running, cities lit, and a genuine sense of occasion. If being *somewhere* for Christmas or New Year's is the point, this is the only option.

The costs are real and stack up: it is peak season across every city on the list, accommodation is at its most expensive and books out earliest, and the crowds are the worst of the winter. Worse, **25 December and 1 January are low-function days almost everywhere** — museums shut, restaurants close or go to fixed holiday menus, and public transport runs reduced timetables. Two of your fourteen days are structurally compromised.

### 2. Early January — after the 6th

The Three Kings celebrations in Spain wrap up on 5–6 January, and immediately afterwards the crowds collapse. This is the quietest, cheapest, easiest-to-book window of the entire year, and museum queues effectively vanish.

The cost is atmosphere. The lights come down, the markets pack up, and you get the cities in their plainest state. It is also the coldest stretch of the winter and, in the northern cities, the darkest.

### 3. Split the difference — arrive around 27 December, run into mid-January

Miss the pre-Christmas price peak, catch New Year's somewhere good, and let the back half of the trip fall into the quiet season. The first few days are still busy; by the second week you have the place largely to yourself.

## My recommendation

**Option 3.** It gets New Year's — the more interesting of the two holidays to spend abroad, and the one with genuinely distinctive local customs like [Madrid's twelve grapes]({{ '/questions/christmas-and-new-years/' | relative_url }}) — while dodging the worst of the Christmas-week crush. And a two-week trip that starts busy and ends quiet has a better shape than one that does the reverse.

It also means only one compromised day (1 January) rather than two.

## What would change my mind

- **If the school calendar doesn't allow it.** This is the constraint that outranks everything here, and we won't know it for a while.
- **If being home for Christmas matters** — then the trip starts on the 26th or later and option 3 becomes option 2, which is fine.
- **If someone specifically wants Christmas markets at their peak** — most wind down right after Christmas, so that pushes toward option 1.
